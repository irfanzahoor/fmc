The console

Batchwell's shop-floor console lives at /fm on your site. It is a separate, deliberately small application: large targets, few fields per screen, and no Desk form to get lost in. Supervisors and finance can still use the full ERPNext Desk — the console does not replace it, and nothing you do in one is hidden from the other.

Signing in

Use your normal staff account. An anonymous visitor is sent to the login page, and a portal (Website User) account is refused outright — the console is for staff only.

Choosing your company

The switcher at the top names the company every screen is reading. If yours is marked not set up, it has no manufacturing policy yet and nothing will work until an administrator creates one. Send them to the Administrator guide.

The company selector is a convenience, not a security boundary. Every request is re-checked on the server, so picking a company you are not permitted to see returns nothing rather than someone else's figures.

Appearance

Five palettes — Batchwell (the default forest green), Midnight Blue, Amethyst, Graphite and Copper — and two font pairings, Studio and Humanist. The choice is stored in your own browser, so it follows you on that device and changes nothing for anyone else.

Chart colours deliberately do not follow the palette. A quality status keeps one meaning on every screen and in every theme, and each one also carries an icon and a word, so colour alone never states anything.

My workspace

/fm/workspace — the screen to open first. It shows the work waiting for your role rather than the whole plant: the panels an owner sees are not the panels a storekeeper sees.

Each panel names what it is counting and links straight to the screen that clears it. A panel with nothing in it says so in words; an empty panel is a good morning, not a broken screen.

Where a figure has never been measured the workspace shows an em dash (—), never a zero. "We did not measure this" and "this is zero" are different facts and the app will not blur them.

Overview

/fm/dashboard — the plant at a glance. Four figures across the top: average actual yield, awaiting quality, near expiry and open work orders. Below them, Needs your attention lists the exceptions worth acting on, then the yield trend, output by item, the stock quality mix and the expiry horizon.

Nothing here is editable. It is a reading screen: every tile and every bar links to the screen where the work actually happens.

Goods in

/fm/receiving — receive stock against a purchase order and capture what the supplier shipped.

  1. Pick the purchase order. Its open lines load underneath.
  2. For each line, enter the receive quantity, the supplier lot number and the supplier's manufacturing date. The lot number is the supplier's own, printed on their bag — not a number you invent.
  3. Press Post receipt.

That creates a standard Purchase Receipt and, for every batch-tracked item, a Batch carrying the supplier lot, the manufacturing date, the expiry date and a quality status of Quarantine. Where the policy requires incoming inspection the stock lands in the quarantine warehouse rather than in stores.

Stock in quarantine cannot be consumed or dispatched. It is not lost — it is waiting for a quality decision, and it will appear in the Quality queue the moment you post the receipt.

If you press the button twice, or the network drops mid-post, you will not get two receipts. The screen sends a request identifier with the post, and a repeat of the same request returns the original document instead of posting again.

Quality

/fm/quality — the release queue, longest-waiting first, with four counters above it: awaiting a decision, in quarantine, currently on hold and released in period.

Deciding on a batch

Select a batch and the panel beside it fills with what you need before deciding: the item, the supplier and their lot number, the manufacturing and expiry dates, how much remaining shelf life is left as a proportion of the whole, the batch's decision history, and its genealogy — what it was made from, or what it has become.

Then record one of three decisions:

DecisionWhat happens
ReleaseStatus becomes Released and the stock moves out of quarantine. It can now be consumed and dispatched.
HoldThe batch stays where it is and stays blocked. Use this when you are waiting on a result rather than refusing the stock.
RejectThe stock moves to the rejected warehouse and can never be consumed. From there it can be returned to the supplier or written off by someone authorised to do it.

Every decision is a submitted record naming who decided, when, and why. It cannot be edited afterwards — a later decision supersedes an earlier one, and both stay on the batch's history.

Inspections

Where a Quality Inspection exists for the batch, its readings are shown beside the decision so you are not reading a result in one window and deciding in another. A reading on a different item or a different batch is not accepted as evidence for this one, and a cancelled inspection cannot support a release.

Inspecting and releasing are different jobs. An FM Quality Inspector records results. Only an FM Quality Manager can release, hold or reject. If the decision form is missing for you, that is the reason.

Production

/fm/production — the open work orders, and the screen that closes them.

  1. Pick a run from Open work orders.
  2. Ingredients & released lots shows what the run needs and which released batches can supply it, earliest-expiring first. A lot that is in quarantine, on hold or rejected is not offered, because it cannot be used.
  3. Under Finish this run, enter the good quantity produced and the process loss — the material that is gone, not material you recovered as a by-product.
  4. Press Post and complete.

The app posts the stock entries, creates the output batch, and works out the yield: planned against actual, with process loss kept separate. A run outside the company's yield tolerance is flagged for review — flagged, not blocked. The run still happened; someone should look at it before the next one.

Quantities are in the item's own unit. If a blend reads "500 Nos" where you expect kilograms, the unit is wrong on the item, not on the run — tell your administrator.

Packaging is a separate run per pack size: it consumes the bulk blend and the packaging materials, never the raw ingredients again. That is what makes it impossible to consume an ingredient twice.

Recipe cost and packaging

/fm/costing — two tools that answer "what should this cost" and "what do I need to buy".

What a batch should cost

Choose a recipe and a batch size. The simulator prices it from the recipe and shows the total per batch broken into ingredients, packaging and operating, with an ingredient-by-ingredient breakdown underneath and the assumptions it used stated openly beside the figure.

This is an estimate priced from the source the BOM declares. It is not posted valuation and is never presented as the ledger's answer. What a batch did cost comes from the ledger, and the two are shown separately on purpose.

What a pack run needs

Enter the pack quantities you intend to run. The planner works out the blend, pouches, labels and cartons required, then nets off free stock, open requests and expected receipts, so what is left is the real shortfall rather than a gross requirement.

Create draft raises a draft Material Request for the shortfall. It is not an order. A buyer reviews and submits it — purchasing authority does not sit behind a shop-floor button.

Dispatch

/fm/dispatch — pick batches against a sales order and raise the delivery note.

Choose the sales order and the lines load with a FEFO suggestion already filled in: the earliest-expiring eligible batch, with held, rejected, expired and too-near-expiry stock excluded. Where the order is in a selling unit and stock is held in another, Sales quantity → stock quantity shows the conversion rather than leaving you to do it.

You can pick a different batch. What happens then is set by your company's policy:

FEFO settingPicking out of order
OffAllowed silently.
WarnAllowed, with a warning naming the batch you skipped.
BlockRefused. Pick the suggested batch, or have the policy changed.

Three things are refused whatever the FEFO setting: an expired batch, a batch below the company's minimum remaining shelf life, and a batch that quality has not released. Create delivery note posts a standard Delivery Note, and every one of those checks runs again on the server as it is submitted.

Recall

/fm/recall — read-only, and open to anyone who can read stock. Give it a batch and it traces in both directions.

  • Forward, from a supplier lot: every batch it became, and every delivery and customer that received it.
  • Backward, from a sold pack: the bulk blend it was filled from, the run that made it, the ingredient batches that run consumed, and the supplier lot behind each one.

The screen reports affected batches, what is still on hand, and customer deliveries and returns. The trace is derived from submitted stock transactions every time you ask, not from a separate record that could quietly fall out of step with the ledger. Cancelled and returned transactions are marked rather than silently dropped.

Recall produces the list of who to contact. It does not contact them. Telling a customer has legal and commercial weight, and stays a human decision.

Insights

Two reading screens for looking back over a period rather than acting now.

  • Production insights (/fm/insights/production) — yield by run, output by item, and the process loss rate.
  • Quality overview (/fm/insights/quality) — stock by quality status, and the expiry horizon.

Process loss is shown as a rate, not a weight. Ranked in kilograms the product you make most of always looks like the worst offender, which is a fact about volume rather than about waste.

The five rules

These are the things the app will stop you doing. All five are enforced on the server, so they hold in the console, in the Desk, over the API and in a data import alike — there is no screen that quietly skips them.

RuleWhat it stops
Quality gateConsuming or dispatching stock that is in quarantine, on hold or rejected. Only Released stock moves.
Shelf lifeDispatching a batch below the company's minimum remaining shelf life. Expired stock is blocked outright.
FEFOPicking out of expiry order — warned or blocked, depending on the policy.
YieldNothing, but a run outside tolerance is flagged for review before the next one.
Recipe confidentialityReading the formula. Ingredient percentages are visible only to an FM Recipe Manager — an operator can run a blend without ever seeing what is in it.

Every threshold in those rules is your company's, set in its policy. The app ships no food-safety values of its own and makes no certification or compliance claim.

The shape of each chart follows the job its data has to do.

Every chart has a table view, works from the keyboard, and respects prefers-reduced-motion. On a phone the yield plot is replaced by its run table rather than squeezed — twelve points in a phone's width is not a readable chart.

When a screen refuses

A refusal names what is missing. There are two kinds, and they need different people.

"You need the … role"

A permission problem. The console's screens read standard ERPNext documents, and Batchwell's own roles deliberately do not widen access to those — so you need both. The message names the role to ask for. Your administrator grants it; see Granting roles.

"This company is not set up"

The company has no manufacturing policy. Nothing on any screen will work until one exists, because every rule reads its thresholds from it.

A blocked action

A refused release, a blocked dispatch or a rejected consumption is the app working, not failing. The message says which batch and which rule. If the rule itself is wrong for your plant, the threshold is configurable — that is a conversation with whoever owns the policy, not something to work around.

Two symptoms that are nearly always a stale build rather than a permission problem: "Insufficient Permission for Company" on the very first load, and the console loading but showing no data at all. Both are in the Administrator guide.
