Install

cd ~/frappe-bench
bench get-app https://github.com/irfanzahoor/fmc --branch version-16
bench --site <your-site> install-app food_manufacturing
bench --site <your-site> migrate
bench build --app food_manufacturing

The install is idempotent: running it again creates nothing twice and overwrites nothing you have changed. bench build matters — the console is a compiled frontend, and most of the odd first-load failures in Troubleshooting are a build that did not run.

It creates no company, no warehouse, no item, no policy and no threshold. Everything the rules key off is yours to set, which is the next three sections.

Company policy

FM Manufacturing Policy — one per company, named after it. This is the single most important record in the app: every rule reads its thresholds from here, and until it exists the console marks the company not set up and no screen works.

These defaults are starting points, not recommendations. The app ships no food-safety thresholds of its own: 25% remaining shelf life and 2% yield tolerance are placeholders for your plant's own numbers, and nobody but you can supply them.

Set up your items

Every stock item carries a Material Nature (fm_material_nature). This is what the rules key off — an item without one is invisible to most of the app.

NatureBatch-trackedUsed for
Raw MaterialyesSpices, salt, oils — anything received and inspected.
Packaging MaterialnoPouches, cartons, labels — counted, not lot-tracked.
Bulk BlendyesThe blended output of a recipe, before packing.
Finished GoodyesA pack size filled from a bulk blend.

A Finished Good also names the bulk item it is filled from (fm_bulk_item), the fill quantity (fm_pack_size) and its unit (fm_pack_uom), so a packaging run can be checked against the blend it came from.

Every batch-tracked item needs Has Batch No, Create New Batch, a batch number series, and a shelf life in days. Without the shelf life the dispatch floor has nothing to measure and quietly stops protecting you.

Recipes and confidentiality

FM Recipe holds the formula as percentages of the blend, which must total 100%. Submit it to approve it; approval generates a standard submitted BOM with explicit quantities and units, and production runs against that BOM.

A recipe change creates a new revision and a new BOM. It never mutates the old one, so historical production keeps the exact formula it was made to — the Work Order pins the submitted BOM, and ERPNext will not let that be edited.

Ingredient percentages sit at permlevel 1. Only FM Recipe Manager can read or write them, and they are stripped from list views, document reads, the export template and the exporter for everyone else. Granting that role is granting the formula.

Granting roles

Batchwell's {{ facts.roles }} roles cover its documents. The console's screens read standard ERPNext documents, and the FM roles deliberately do not widen access to those. So an operator needs a role from each column.

Three that surprise people: Batch is readable by Item Manager alone on a stock site; Quality Inspection by ERPNext's own Quality Manager alone, which is why a batch's matching inspection will not load without it; and Company by no FM role at all.

Because Company is unreadable to FM roles, the console derives its company list from FM Manufacturing Policy instead, which every FM role can read. That is why an account holding only FM roles can still open the app.

The near-expiry alert

A daily scheduled job emails batches coming up on their expiry date, using each company's near_expiry_alert_days horizon. It goes to users holding FM Quality Manager, FM Production Manager or FM Warehouse Operator.

It needs the scheduler to be running and outgoing email to be configured. Neither is something the app can arrange for you, and with either missing the job simply does nothing.

This is the only mail the app sends, and it goes to your own staff. Batchwell never notifies a customer about anything — including a recall.

Demo data

bench --site <site> execute food_manufacturing.demo.seed.create_demo_data \
  --kwargs '{"company":"Your Company (Demo)"}'

bench --site <site> execute food_manufacturing.demo.seed.remove_demo_data \
  --kwargs '{"company":"Your Company (Demo)"}'

The seeder creates real ERPNext documents, so the demo exercises the same validations a plant would. Everything is prefixed FM Demo, scoped to one company, and safe to run twice.

It deliberately leaves work open on every screen — two work orders to complete, a purchase order to receive, a sales order to pick, one lot still in quarantine and one rejected. A demo where every queue is empty reads as a broken app.

Never seed a production company. Two further cautions: the seeder turns on Stock Settings.enable_serial_and_batch_no_for_item, which affects every company on the site (teardown restores it only if the seeder changed it); and demo data in the company your tests discover will make company-wide assertions fail.

Upgrading

cd ~/frappe-bench/apps/food_manufacturing
git pull
cd ~/frappe-bench
bench --site <site> migrate
bench build --app food_manufacturing
bench --site <site> clear-cache

migrate re-asserts the roles, custom fields and modules, so a field removed by hand comes back. build is not optional: the console is compiled, and a stale build is the cause of most of what follows.

After changing any Python in the app on a development bench, clear-cache is not enough — restart the server. A stale worker serving old code looks exactly like a bug that will not fix.

Troubleshooting

"Insufficient Permission for Company" on first load

An account holding only FM roles cannot read Company. The console works around this by deriving its company list from the policy — so seeing this message means the browser is running an old build. Run yarn build (or bench build --app food_manufacturing) and clear-cache.

The console loads but shows nothing

The company has no FM Manufacturing Policy; the switcher marks it not set up. Create one — see Company policy.

CSRFTokenError: Invalid Request

The page shell did not supply boot data, so the frontend sent every request without a CSRF token. The app loads perfectly and shows no data. Rebuild and clear the cache.

A batch's matching inspection will not load

Quality Inspection is readable by ERPNext's Quality Manager role alone. Grant it.

A blend reads "500 Nos" instead of kilograms

Work Order.stock_uom is only populated by the Desk form. The app reads the unit from the Item instead, so if you see this anywhere, the unit on the Item is what to check.

The near-expiry email never arrives

Check, in order: the scheduler is enabled on the site, outgoing email is configured, at least one user holds one of the three notified roles, and the company's near_expiry_alert_days horizon actually covers a batch.
