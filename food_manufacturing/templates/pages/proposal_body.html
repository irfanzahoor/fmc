The problem this solves

A spice plant runs on facts ERPNext does not model on its own. Which supplier lot went into which blend. Whether a batch has been released by quality or is still sitting in quarantine. How much blend survived the grinder. Which customers received stock from a lot you now need back.

Most plants answer those in spreadsheets kept beside the ERP, which means the answer and the ledger disagree the moment anyone is busy. Batchwell puts the answers in the ledger.

What it does

CapabilityWhat it means on the floor
Supplier lotsEvery receipt records the supplier's own lot number and manufacturing date, and routes to quarantine when policy requires inspection.
Confidential recipesFormulas are held as percentages behind a permission level. An operator can run a blend without ever seeing what is in it.
Quality hold and releaseQuarantine, hold, release or reject — every decision audited with who decided and why. Unreleased stock cannot be consumed or dispatched, enforced on the server.
Production and yieldActual output and non-recoverable process loss per run, with runs outside tolerance flagged for review before the next one.
Packaging planningBlend, pouches, labels and cartons for a planned pack quantity, with free stock, open requests and expected receipts netted off.
Shelf life and FEFODispatch suggests the earliest-expiring eligible batch and blocks stock below your minimum remaining shelf life.
Traceability and recallTrace a supplier lot forward to every batch it became and every customer who received it — and back again — from submitted stock transactions.
CostingWhat a batch would cost from the recipe, against what it did cost from the ledger, with the gap per run.

How it is built

Standard ERPNext underneath

Receipts are Purchase Receipts. Runs are Work Orders and Stock Entries. Dispatch is a Delivery Note. There is no parallel stock ledger and no second valuation: accounting behaves exactly as it does in ERPNext, because it is ERPNext.

Rules on the server

Every rule is enforced in document events, so it holds for the Desk form, the REST API and a Data Import alike. A rule that only lives in a screen is not a rule.

Your limits, not ours

Inspection parameters, acceptance ranges, shelf-life floors, FEFO strictness and yield tolerance are all configured per company. This app ships no food-safety thresholds of its own and makes no certification or compliance claim.

An operator console

A dedicated workspace at /fm for the shop floor — built for gloved hands and tablet screens — while supervisors and finance keep the full ERPNext Desk.

What we would need from you

  1. A company set up in ERPNext — chart of accounts, warehouses, and the items you buy and sell.
  2. Your quality parameters — what you test, and the acceptance range for each. These become Quality Inspection Templates; we do not supply values.
  3. Your recipes — ingredient percentages per blend, which we load as confidential formulas.
  4. Your pack definitions — fill quantity per SKU and the packaging each one consumes.
  5. Policy decisions — minimum remaining shelf life, whether FEFO warns or blocks, and your yield tolerance.

Being straight about scope

A proposal that lists only strengths is not much use for planning, so these are the current limits:

  • Targets Frappe {{ facts.frappe_version }} and ERPNext {{ facts.erpnext_version }} only. Earlier majors are out of scope.
  • The console is light-themed. There is no dark mode.
  • Recall reports affected batches, stock on hand and customer deliveries. It does not notify customers — who to contact is produced; the contacting stays a human decision.
  • The packaging planner proposes a draft Material Request. Nothing is ordered without a buyer.
  • Costing is an estimate priced from the source the BOM declares. It is never presented as posted valuation.
